Safety

Six rules it cannot break

Enforced in code and in CI, not in a policy document. The only way FileMind can delete anything is to move it to the Trash, and CI fails the build if a hard delete appears in the core.

1Never delete permanently

Trash is the only deletion primitive. There is no rm in the core, and the lint that proves it runs on every commit.

2Never mass-move without a manifest

Every change is a transaction: written and synced to disk before step one, replayable after a crash, reversible after.

3Never overwrite

A move whose destination exists is refused. Conflicts get a visible name and a note — never a silent rename.

4Never silently rename

Every rename is a step in the plan you approve. What you saw in the preview is what runs, checked by fingerprint.

5Never follow unknown links

Symlinks and junctions are recorded, not traversed. Nothing outside the folders you chose is ever read or moved.

6Never touch the OS or app internals

System folders, app bundles and Library are protected at the scanner, the planner and the adapter — three layers deep.

Privacy

Local by default. Explicit about the exceptions.

Stays on your Mac

  • The index, the content hashes, the extracted text
  • The embedding model and every vector it produces
  • The transaction journal and your undo history
  • The database itself — encrypted at rest, key in your Keychain

What an AI adapter sees

  • Nothing, until you turn one on in Settings
  • For a question: file names, folders, dates and up to 2 KB of text
  • Never a full file, never a path outside your chosen folders
  • Never a file marked sensitive (keys, .env, card and ID numbers)

The audit log

  • Every call to any adapter — local Ollama or cloud
  • Which model, when, how many bytes, and whether it left the machine
  • Ollama “cloud” models are labelled CLOUD, not local
  • Viewable in the app; the text itself is never stored

Details, including what opt-in telemetry sends if you enable it, are in the privacy page.
